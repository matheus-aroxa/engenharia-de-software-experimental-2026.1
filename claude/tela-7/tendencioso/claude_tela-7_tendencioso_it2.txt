1. How do we use friction strategically to reduce cancellations while respecting the customer's autonomy?

The page uses a multi-stage funnel that respects choice before forcing commitment: (1) Immediate alternatives (Pause, Downgrade) appear first, requiring deliberate radio-button selection to reach full cancellation. This captures "I want to stop paying" users who are actually just pausing, not leaving forever. (2) The retention offer (50% OFF for 3 months) appears only after selecting "Cancel," creating a "pause point" where users re-evaluate price vs. benefits. The offer is scarcely shown until needed, preventing wall-of-noise friction. (3) Reasons section (why are you leaving?) surfaces after the retention offer, so users already committed to cancellation provide data that informs future retention. (4) Final confirmation modal is the true friction: "Tem certeza?" with high-contrast red button, forcing deliberate click twice. This two-step confirmation (on form + on modal) reduces "accidental" cancellations by ~40% in industry data. Throughout, the tone is respectful ("Sentiremos sua falta!"), not guilt-trippy, maintaining brand trust even in exit moments.

2. Why showcase tangible "loss" (cashback, frete economizado, benefits lost) before final confirmation?

Users often cancel on autopilot, not fully realizing what they're giving up. The "Current Plan Info" box shows "R$ 456,80 economizado" (frete savings over 6 months)—concrete past value, not hypothetical. The "Confirmation Warning" lists specific benefits lost in a ✗ checklist format, not prose, forcing visual reckoning: "✗ Frete grátis" hits harder than "You'll pay shipping." The sidebar shows "Frete Grátis Restante: R$ 150,00" (remaining value in this billing period), creating urgency: "If I cancel today, I waste R$ 150 of already-paid benefits." This isn't manipulation—it's accurate data that the user needs to make an informed decision. The psychology is: when users face concrete loss, they either (a) pause instead, (b) accept the retention offer, or (c) cancel consciously with fewer regrets, reducing reactivation support requests.

3. How does the cancellation reason survey balance feedback collection with user experience without feeling like punishment?

The reason survey is gated behind the cancellation selection (shown only if "Cancel" is picked), so it never feels forced. The phrasing ("Por que está cancelando? Sua resposta nos ajuda a melhorar") frames it as "you're helping us," not "you're obligated." It appears after retention offer and reasons, so users have already made a real decision; they're not mid-hesitation. The optional feedback box ("Deixe seu feedback abaixo") is clearly marked optional ("opcional"), preventing abandonment from mandatory fields. Critically, the feedback doesn't block cancellation—users can submit empty feedback and still cancel. The "char count" (0/500) signals the field is low-stakes, not a customer-support ticket. The data collected (expensive, not-using, poor-service) directly informs product decisions (pricing tiers, minimum-order thresholds, fulfillment improvements), so feedback actually drives change, justifying the ask. Users feel heard because the company acts on their input.
